# Nashville building permits: among permits issued in the last three years, the median entry-to-issue wait was 29 days, and a Commercial Tenant Finish Out permit waited a median of 119 days

**One-line finding:** Among the 29,651 building permits issued in Nashville from October 30, 2023 to November 2, 2026, the median time from application entry to issuance was 29 days; Commercial Tenant Finish Out permits (1,573 permits) waited a median of 119 days, and Council District 19 holds $3.15 billion of the $13.23 billion in applicant-stated construction value (23.8 percent) with a median wait of 62 days.

Source: Metro Nashville open data, Building Permits Issued layer (Building_Permits_Issued_2, 29,651 records pulled November 3, 2026), maintained from the Metro Department of Codes and Building Safety ePermits/CityWorks systems. One row is one ISSUED building permit. The layer's own metadata (Building Permits Issued Metadata v2.0, November 12, 2024) states that the dataset does not contain applications (including applications never issued) or trade permits, and that Construction Cost is the stated value of construction reported by the applicant.

Four definitions govern everything below. First, every wait in this brief is a wait among permits issued in the window. The dataset contains no pending or never-issued applications, so there is no pending-application denominator in this dataset and no figure here describes how long applicants in general wait; applications still pending are absent by design, and waits for recent entry cohorts are right-censored (see Data notes). Second, every cost figure is stated value: the construction cost the applicant reported in the permit record, printed as reported, never as a measured or audited figure. Third, counts are permits, not projects: multi-unit developments issue one child permit per dwelling unit under a master permit, so a single development can be many rows. Fourth, the window is the layer's rolling three years by issue date, October 30, 2023 to November 2, 2026 (America/Chicago); entry dates run earlier, back to June 17, 2020, because long-pending permits issued inside the window are included.

## Finding 1: Among issued permits, the median wait was 29 days, and 1 in 5 issued permits (20.4 percent) took more than 90 days

The layer holds 29,651 permits issued in the window (server count and local count equal at the November 3, 2026 pull). Timing is computed on the 29,650 permits with a valid wait; one served row carries a negative wait (-11 days, a Building Demolition Permit) and is excluded from timing and counted here and in the audit. On the valid population, the median entry-to-issue wait was 29 days, the mean was 64.9 days, and the 90th percentile was 166 days. The distribution is: same day, 754 permits (2.5 percent); 1-30 days, 14,342 (48.4 percent); 31-90 days, 8,494 (28.6 percent); 91-180 days, 3,431 (11.6 percent); 181-365 days, 2,049 (6.9 percent); over 365 days, 580 (2.0 percent). In total, 6,060 issued permits (20.4 percent) waited more than 90 days and 2,629 (8.9 percent) waited more than 180 days.

## Finding 2: Waits differ by permit class - Commercial Tenant Finish Out waited a median of 119 days, Tree Removal a median of 0 days

By permit type (median wait, timed permits): Building Commercial - Tenant Finish Out, 119 days (1,573 permits; 58.7 percent of them waited more than 90 days); Building Commercial - New, 93 days (864; 51.2 percent over 90 days); Building Commercial - Rehab, 50 days (3,793); Building Residential - New, 35 days (10,954, the largest class at 36.9 percent of all permits in the window); Building Residential - Addition, 22 days (3,167); Building Demolition Permit, 14 days (2,170 timed of 2,171 rows; the excluded negative-wait row is in this class); Building Sign Permit, 14 days (1,989); Building Residential - Rehab, 8 days (2,430); Building Tree Removal Permit, 0 days (325; 56.3 percent issued the same day). Two smaller classes record higher medians than Tenant Finish Out and are printed with their counts: Building Commercial - Shell, 150 days (149 permits), and Building Use & Occupancy, 111 days (389 permits).

The pattern across classes is the finding: the classes with same-day or single-digit medians are the low-review classes (Tree Removal, Residential Rehab, Change Contractor classes), and the classes above 90 days at the median are commercial build-out, new commercial, shell, and use-and-occupancy classes. The Tree Removal median of 0 days is this package's internal control: a simple class does not sit in a long queue, so the long commercial medians are not a uniform system-wide queue artifact (control recorded in verification.md).

## Finding 3: Permits issued in the window carry $13.23 billion of applicant-stated value, and District 19 holds 23.8 percent of it

Stated value across the window totals $13,227,134,850 (applicant-reported; median permit $102,205, mean $446,094, single maximum $226,068,759; no permit in the pull carries a zero, null, or negative stated value). Council district is known on 29,632 of 29,651 permits (unknown on 19: 2 rows with district 0, a sentinel, and 17 rows null). On known-district permits, the five districts with the most stated value are: District 19, $3,148,570,433 across 2,514 permits; District 2, $892,808,044 (1,482 permits); District 18, $884,661,966 (957); District 17, $804,497,673 (1,576); and District 20, $676,523,843 (1,255). District 19's $3.15 billion is 23.8 percent of the citywide stated total, and the top five districts together hold 48.6 percent of the known-district stated total.

By ZIP code, the largest permit counts are 37207 (2,698 permits, $1,118,501,549 stated), 37209 (2,572 permits, $850,292,830 stated), and 37203 (2,398 permits, $2,461,416,239 stated, 18.6 percent of the citywide stated total). Census tract is carried in mixed formats in the layer; after the documented normalization recorded in verification.md, 29,629 of 29,651 permits (99.9 percent) carry a Davidson County census tract GEOID across 182 tracts, so tract-level tabulation is reproducible from this package's cleaned layer.

## Finding 4: District 19's median wait (62 days) is about double the citywide median, and its permit mix is 68.7 percent commercial against 25.0 percent citywide

District 19's median entry-to-issue wait among issued permits is 62 days, against the citywide median of 29 days. The other high-stated-value districts sit closer to the citywide figure: District 2, 49 days; District 17, 37.5 days; District 20, 36 days; District 18, 33 days. These district medians are unadjusted. District 19's permit mix differs from the city's: 68.7 percent of District 19's issued permits are in commercial classes (permit type descriptions containing Commercial), against 25.0 percent citywide (package QA recomputation from the cleaned layer, recorded in verification.md), and commercial classes wait longer everywhere (Finding 2). Within single classes, District 19 medians on this pull are also above the citywide class medians: Commercial Tenant Finish Out, 153 days in District 19 (572 permits) against 119 citywide; Commercial Rehab, 72 days (859 permits) against 50; Residential New, 52 days (148 permits) against 35. Both facts are printed because both are in the data: the mix difference is large, and it does not by itself account for the within-class differences, which are themselves unadjusted for project size and stated value.

## Finding 5: Permit counts are permits and units, not projects - 29,600 unique permit numbers and 20,851 parcels sit behind the 29,651 rows

Permit numbers repeat on 51 rows (29,600 unique permit numbers across 29,651 rows), and the rows name 20,851 unique parcels. The Purpose text, where present, shows the mechanism: multi-unit developments issue one child permit per dwelling unit under a master permit. Purpose is null on 296 rows (1.0 percent). Every count in this brief is therefore a count of permits (in multi-unit cases, of permitted units), and no figure here is a count of projects or developments. Two field-level notes bound the type figures: the Permit_Type_Description field is authoritative in this layer, and its full 29-value Counter is recorded in the audit; the parallel Per_Ty code field disagrees with the Description counts on small numbers and holds a person's name on 1 row, so no figure uses the code field (recorded in stats.json).

## What this might mean

One reading of Findings 2 and 4 together is that the review queue is class-shaped. Simple classes issue at a median of 0-8 days while the tenant finish-out class - the permit a business typically needs before it can fit out and open a space - waits a median of 119 days among issued permits, with 58.7 percent waiting more than 90 days. On that reading, class-level queue reporting (median wait by permit class, published from this same layer on its daily refresh) is the measure that would show whether the commercial build-out waits move, and the tables in this package are the baseline.

The strongest alternative explanation applies to Finding 4 and tempers Finding 2: composition. District 19's permits are 68.7 percent commercial against 25.0 percent citywide, and its largest classes are Commercial Rehab (859 permits) and Tenant Finish Out (572). A district whose mix skews to large commercial projects would record a longer median wait even if every class were processed at the citywide pace. The within-class medians printed in Finding 4 narrow that explanation without closing it: they are not adjusted for stated value, project size, or subtype, any of which could differ between District 19 and the city within a class. For Finding 1, the alternative is the issuance condition itself: because only issued permits are observed, the 29-day median is a median over completed waits, and applications that are still pending - including any that will wait far longer - are not in the average at all.

## Data notes

- Snapshot and vintage: all figures are from the November 3, 2026 pull (29,651 records, server count equal to local count, immutable snapshot in the project warehouse). The same layer held 29,661 records at the October 30, 2026 shelf-ready study pull this brief refreshes, a drift of -10 records in four days as the rolling window moved (issued window October 26, 2023-October 29, 2026 in the study; October 30, 2023-November 2, 2026 here). The study's headline medians moved with the window: the all-permit median wait was 30 days in the study and is 29 days here; total stated value was $13,199,129,583 in the study and is $13,227,134,850 here. Figures describe the layer as pulled on November 3, 2026.
- No pending-application denominator: the metadata states this dataset does not contain applications, including ones never issued. There is therefore no base of all applications against which an issuance rate or an all-applicant wait could be computed from this dataset, and this brief computes none. Entry-to-issue waits are observed only for permits that were issued; recent entry cohorts are right-censored, so no cohort median for recent quarters is printed.
- Dates and times: both date fields are served as epochs that are local (America/Chicago) midnights - the audit records that the only UTC hours present are 5 and 6 - and every date figure reads them in America/Chicago. Exact window: issued October 30, 2023 to November 2, 2026; entered June 17, 2020 to October 30, 2026.
- Category stability: a full Counter of Permit_Type_Description (29 values, summing to 29,651) was recorded before any figure was computed (audit output). No category rename or definition change inside the window is documented in the metadata (Building Permits Issued Metadata v2.0, November 12, 2024). The code-field discrepancy (Per_Ty) is recorded in Finding 5.
- Unit discipline: rows are permits throughout. Every share's numerator and denominator are permits over permits on a named population (all rows, valid-wait rows, or known-district rows), asserted in the audit. Stated value is applicant-reported wherever it appears.
- Joins: no dataset join is used. Geography is the layer's own council district and ZIP fields. The one transformation is the census tract normalization (11-digit GEOID form as served; 8-digit form prefixed to a 9-digit zero-padded county-plus-tract form), which places 29,629 of 29,651 rows (99.93 percent) on a Davidson County GEOID; 22 rows stay unknown (17 null, 3 short codes, 2 anomalies that do not resolve to a Davidson County GEOID). The control - no parcel maps to more than one normalized tract, and the two served forms share 118 tracts - is recorded in verification.md.
- Small numbers: percentages are printed with their underlying counts throughout. Classes under about 30 timed permits (the smallest is Building Moving Permit, 3 permits) are in the data panels with their counts and are used in no finding; the smallest class cited in a finding is Building Commercial - Shell at 149 permits, printed with its count.
- Official definitions: field meanings are the metadata's own (Building Permits Issued Metadata v2.0, archived in this package as data/source-metadata.pdf): one record is an issued building permit; Date Entered is the date applied for; Date Issued is the date approved and issued; Construction Cost is the stated value of construction; coverage is a rolling three years, updated daily, extracted from ePermits/CityWorks.
- Named records: this brief names no individual person, address, parcel, or permit. District and ZIP figures are aggregates over hundreds to thousands of records. Contact and address values stay in the warehouse layer only.
- Reproduction: code/pull_data.py records the pull (data/pull_log.json); code/audit_section10.py records the pre-flight audit (data/audit_output.txt); code/build_stats.py builds stats.json and the cleaned warehouse layer; code/verify_independent.py is the independent recomputation (data/verify_independent_output.txt, 9 of 9 checks passed); code/charts.py draws the charts; code/hero_composite.py composites the hero from stats.json and hero_source/hero-art.webp.
